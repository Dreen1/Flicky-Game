# Lean Canvas: Flick Battle (game collection)

Last updated: November 11, 2026 · Beta target: TestFlight, November 17, 2026

## 1. Problem
- Friends and families sharing one phone or tablet have few good **head-to-head** games. Most mobile games are solo.
- Casual games are packed with forced ads and pay-to-win offers, so parents don't trust them for kids.
- Simple skill games where **the better player actually wins** are rare. Many popular games quietly rig difficulty to sell revives.

**What people use today:** carrom and pool-style flick games (Soccer Stars, Carrom Pool), pass-and-play board game apps, and pen-and-paper games like the one this started from.

## 2. Customer segments
- **Families and pairs on one device:** parents and kids, couples, friends. *Primary for the beta.*
- **Solo casual players:** a few minutes against the computer.
- **Competitive players:** later, once online play and leaderboards exist.

**Early adopters:** parents with kids 8 and up, church and school communities, and anyone who played pencil-flick games on paper growing up.

## 3. Unique value proposition
**The pencil-flick battle game from the back of the church bulletin, reborn in space.**
One flick, one shot, and skill decides who wins. No forced ads, safe for the whole family.

*High-level concept:* "Paper football meets Asteroids."

## 4. Solution
- **Flick Battle:** flick your ship through enemy ships. 2 players on one device, or 1 player against the computer. Rounds take 3–5 minutes.
- **Word Battle** (after the beta): a head-to-head word game in the same app.
- **Kids' word game** (after the beta): a gentle word game for young children.
- No forced ads, no rigged difficulty, short rounds.

## 5. Channels
- **App Store search:** a keyword-friendly name (e.g. "Flick Battle: Space Ship Duel"), with the subtitle, description and screenshot text written around what people search.
- **Localized store pages** (Spanish, Portuguese, German, Japanese, ...). The game has very little text, so this is cheap.
- **TestFlight beta** with family, church and community groups, which brings early reviews at launch.
- **Short trick-shot videos** of wrap-around double kills.
- **Free browser demo** that links to the app.
- **Asking for a review after a win.** Ask everyone, and never filter who sees the prompt.

## 6. Revenue streams
- **Free download + one-time "Full Game" unlock at $3.99:** every mode, all ships and arenas.
- **Later:** cosmetic ship-skin packs.
- **No ads**, because of the kids' content and low ad revenue per player.
- Purchases sit behind a **parental gate**, as Apple requires wherever kids can reach them.

## 7. Cost structure
- Apple Developer Program: $99/year (needed for TestFlight and the App Store)
- Google Play: $25 one time (Android, after iOS)
- Art and sound packs: about $20–150 (optional)
- Tools: Claude subscription, Mac and Xcode (already owned)
- Time: evenings around the main business

## 8. Key metrics
- **The one number:** % of matches finished. People quitting mid-match means something's broken or unfun.
- Average round length (target: 3–5 minutes)
- Day 1 and day 7 return rate
- Free-to-paid unlock rate
- App Store rating and number of reviews

## 9. Unfair advantage
- **A true origin story:** a game played with Mom at the Kingdom Hall, finally built after 20 years. People share stories, not features.
- **A mechanic nobody owns:** a forward pencil-flick with a 20° curve and screen wrap. It isn't a pull-back slingshot like the competitors.
- **A family-safe collection** (flick, word battle, kids' words) in one trusted app, with no ads.
